01 · Foundations

What is a projectile?

A projectile is an object given an initial velocity that then moves through the air under the influence of gravity alone, when air resistance is neglected. Its motion is called projectile motion.

Definition

Projectile motion

The curved, two-dimensional motion of an object launched into the air. Near Earth's surface, its ideal path is a parabola.

Examples

Everyday examples

  • A ball thrown at an angle
  • A basketball shot toward a hoop
  • Water flowing from a fountain
  • A long-jump athlete in flight
Important: After reaching its highest point, an object is still a projectile while it falls.
02 · The two motions

One journey, two independent directions

Horizontal motion

In the ideal model, horizontal acceleration is zero and horizontal velocity stays constant.

aₓ = 0 · vₓ = u cos θ

Vertical motion

Gravity acts downward, so vertical acceleration is −g if upward is positive.

aᵧ = −g · vᵧ = u sin θ − gt

Both motions happen at the same time and share the same elapsed time.

03 · Formula toolkit

Important projectile equations

For launch speed u at angle θ above horizontal, from level ground, with no air resistance:

Initial velocity components

uₓ = u cos θ
uᵧ = u sin θ

Position after time t

x = (u cos θ)t
y = (u sin θ)t − ½gt²

Velocity after time t

vₓ = u cos θ
vᵧ = u sin θ − gt

At the highest point

vᵧ = 0
t↑ = (u sin θ)/g

Only the vertical component is zero for an instant.

Total time of flight

T = (2u sin θ)/g

Valid when landing height equals launch height.

Maximum height and range

H = (u² sin² θ)/(2g)
R = (u² sin 2θ)/g
Use g ≈ 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface (some school problems use 10 m/s²). Keep units consistent.

06 · Worked example

Solve it step by step

Question: A ball is projected from level ground at 20 m/s at 30° to the horizontal. Find its time of flight, maximum height and horizontal range. Take g = 10 m/s².

1Given values

u = 20 m/s, θ = 30°, g = 10 m/s²

2Time of flight
T = 2u sin θ / g = (2 × 20 × 0.5) / 10 = 2 s
3Maximum height
H = u² sin² θ / 2g = (400 × 0.25) / 20 = 5 m
4Horizontal range
R = u² sin 2θ / g = 400 × sin 60° / 10 ≈ 34.6 m
Final answers: Time = 2 s; maximum height = 5 m; range ≈ 34.6 m.
07 · Key ideas

Facts worth remembering

Parabolic path

Horizontal position changes uniformly with time while vertical position changes quadratically.

Complementary angles

For the same launch speed and level landing, θ and (90° − θ) give the same range in the ideal model.

Maximum range

On level ground, range is greatest at 45° for a fixed launch speed, ignoring air resistance.

Mass does not appear

In the ideal model, objects launched with the same initial velocity have the same trajectory regardless of mass.

Limits: Real objects experience air resistance and may be affected by wind, spin and air density.
